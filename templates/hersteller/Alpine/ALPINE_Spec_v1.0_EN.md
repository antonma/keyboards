# Alpine — Surface, Materials, Build Notes

**Subset Alpine v1.0** | ISO-DE 75%, 93 caps | Cherry profile | 2026-05-19

The Alpine set is automotive-inspired: clean polished body paint as the main surface, anthracite "carbon controls", a glowing brake-light red as the signature accent. Layout, glyphs, colors, and signature artwork are in the attached Affinity `.af` source and the PDF export — please pick those up from the file.

This spec only covers what the export **doesn't** show: surface finishes, material feel, and the build context around the keycap set.

---

## Surface finishes per zone

The look-and-feel of each zone is part of the design — please choose whatever material (ABS, PBT, or your shop's best option for each finish) gets closest to these surfaces. Legends should be embedded in the cap, not surface-printed, so they survive the topcoats.

- **Schneeweiß (white caps)** — high-gloss clear-lacquer topcoat over the white base. Reference: **BMW Alpinweiß III (code 300)** — polished sports-car body-paint feel, crisp specular highlights, not matte. UV-stabilized.
- **Bremslicht (red caps — Esc, ISO-Enter, blank brand cap)** — high-gloss red lacquer with **visible depth**, slightly orange-leaning. Reference: the polished red lens of a 2010s automotive LED tail-light at dusk. This is the signature element — please make these as visually rich as your process allows. **If your shop can do a translucent or semi-translucent jelly variant in red** (so the cap picks up some light and feels luminous rather than flat), we'd be very interested — let us know what's feasible and at what surcharge.
- **Carbon (anthracite caps — F-row + modifiers)** — fine-matte satin finish, **explicitly not glossy**. The dark anthracite should keep depth and absorb light, not mirror it. The contrast between glossy white/red and matte carbon is the whole point.
- **Aschrosa (LShift + Menu)** — semi-gloss, sitting tonally between the glossy white and the matte carbon.

If any of these finishes is hard to deliver reliably at your production scale, we'd much rather hear "this is doable but adds X" or "we'd suggest Y instead" now than discover problems after tooling.
